---
title: Electoral technology: the solutions transforming elections
---
Electoral technology has become a central component of modern democratic processes. Its scope goes far beyond voting terminals: it encompasses all the tools and systems applied to manage, execute and control an election, from the preparation of the electoral roll to the publication of results.

For electoral authorities, incorporating electoral technology responds to concrete operational needs: faster processes, fewer errors, greater traceability and the ability to deliver results to citizens within reasonable timeframes. But it also responds to a democratic demand: that every stage of the process be transparent, auditable and verifiable.

This article explains what electoral technology encompasses, what problems it solves, how it applies at each stage of an election cycle and what authorities evaluating the modernization of their processes should consider.

## What is electoral technology

Electoral technology is the set of technological solutions designed to optimize the different stages of a voting process. Its goal is to make elections more efficient, accessible and reliable, while maintaining the standards of transparency and control that democracy demands.

The concept covers three broad areas of application.

### Pre-electoral management

It includes the preparation and administration of the voter roll, the training of polling station authorities, the configuration of the electoral offer (candidacies, lists, alliances) and the logistics of material distribution. Digitizing this stage reduces planning errors and allows electoral authorities to operate with greater predictability.

### Election day

It comprises voter check-in at the station, the casting of the ballot (in person or remote) and the operational control of the day. Technology at this stage seeks to streamline the voter experience, minimize errors in participation records and facilitate the work of polling station authorities.

### Post-electoral

It covers the count, the transmission of results, the publication of preliminary data and the audit and verification processes. It is the stage where citizens expect quick answers, and where errors or delays have the greatest impact on public trust.

## The problems electoral technology solves

Traditional electoral processes face operational challenges that repeat across jurisdictions worldwide. Electoral technology offers specific answers to each of them.

### Counting delays

Manual vote counting and tally sheet transcription are slow, error-prone processes. In elections with many stations, categories and lists, the preliminary count can stretch for hours or days. Digitizing the count allows preliminary results to be published in a fraction of that time.

### Logistical complexity

Printing, distributing and restocking party ballots is a costly operation vulnerable to incidents. The theft or shortage of one party's ballots can alter the conditions of competition. Electronic single ballot systems eliminate this variable by using a single blank ballot for all voters.

### Human error

Manual transcription of tally sheets, voter registration and data entry at computing centers are frequent sources of error. Digitizing these processes reduces mistakes and improves data consistency across the entire chain.

### Low participation

Voters residing abroad, people with reduced mobility and voters in hard-to-reach areas face real barriers to exercising their right to vote. Internet voting offers a complementary channel that broadens participation possibilities without compromising the security of the process.

### Lack of traceability

In traditional models, the data chain between the polling station and the publication of results includes multiple intermediaries. Each additional link is a point where traceability can weaken. Electoral technology shortens that chain and documents every step in an auditable way.

## Solutions by stage: how electoral technology is applied

A complete electoral process has multiple stages, and each presents specific challenges that technology can address. The most effective approach is to have solutions that cover the full cycle in an integrated way, rather than solving each stage in isolation.

### Polling station and roll management

Voter check-in is the first step of election day. In the traditional model, the polling station authority looks up the voter in a printed roll, verifies their identity and records their participation manually. This process generates lines, transcription errors and difficulties consolidating participation data in real time.

Notebox, COMITIA's polling station management solution, digitizes this stage through fast reading of the identity document, instant verification in the electronic roll, digital recording of participation and automatic generation of receipts and station documents. This speeds up check-in, improves operational control and allows the central electoral authority to monitor participation in a consolidated way.

### Verifiable in-person voting

Casting the ballot is the central stage of the process. The Electronic Single Ballot (BUE) allows the voter to select their vote on a terminal with a touchscreen, which prints it on a paper ballot and records it on an RFID chip. The voter verifies their ballot before depositing it in the box. The terminal does not retain the vote.

This model combines the speed of digital recording with the verifiability of paper. It minimizes casting errors, eliminates party ballot logistics problems and enables faster, more accurate counting. The dual backup allows cross-audits between the printed and digital records.

### Remote voting

For contexts where in-person requirements limit participation, Invote allows voters to vote securely from any device with an internet connection.

The vote is encrypted on the voter's device and remains encrypted until the moment of counting. The system guarantees ballot secrecy, vote integrity and the individual and universal verifiability of the process.

This modality is especially relevant for diaspora elections, internal organizational processes and consultations seeking to broaden the reach of citizen participation.

### Accessibility

Electoral technology plays an active role in widening access to voting for people with disabilities. BUE includes an assisted voting module for voters with visual or motor disabilities. Invote was recognized at the Zero Project Conference (Essl Foundation, United Nations) for its contribution to electoral accessibility, allowing people with visual or physical disabilities, or with literacy-related needs, to vote without assistance. The public results portals of Notebox and Turing are also built to W3C accessibility standards.

### Counting, transmission and publication of results

Once voting closes, the speed and accuracy of the count determine the electoral authority's ability to inform citizens. Turing digitizes the preliminary count from the polling place itself: tally sheets are scanned, interpreted and validated by the polling station authority before party overseers, then transmitted in encrypted form to a computing center.

This process eliminates the intermediation of physically transporting tally sheets and manual data entry, drastically reducing publication times and transcription errors. Turing operates both in elections with electronic ballots and in elections with manual voting.

## Security and trust in electoral technology

Modernizing an electoral process only makes sense if it does not compromise public trust. Electoral authorities evaluating the incorporation of technology must verify that the solutions meet fundamental security principles.

Auditability requires that systems be reviewable by independent third parties, and that processes be open to oversight by political parties. Verifiability requires that the voter have mechanisms to confirm that their vote was correctly recorded, and that auditors can verify results universally. Contingency plans must ensure operational continuity in the face of technical incidents, without affecting votes already cast.

International certifications are a concrete indicator that the provider operates under verifiable standards. ## Comprehensive modernization of the electoral process

Electoral technology has its greatest impact when applied comprehensively, covering all stages of the cycle with solutions that complement each other.

An electoral authority that digitizes polling station management but keeps a manual preliminary count solves only part of the problem. Likewise, implementing electronic voting without a results transmission solution leaves an operational gap at the stage where citizens most need answers.

COMITIA addresses this reality with an ecosystem of interconnected solutions: Notebox for polling station and roll management, BUE for verifiable in-person voting, Invote for remote voting and Turing for counting and transmission of results.

Each solution can be implemented independently according to the jurisdiction's needs, but their greatest value is achieved when they operate in a coordinated way within the same electoral process.
